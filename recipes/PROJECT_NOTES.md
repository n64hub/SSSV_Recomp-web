## Executive Summary

For decades, the N64 recompilation community has asked the wrong question.
The question was never *"Can we run Space Station Silicon Valley natively at 60fps?"*
The question was always *"Can we get cake out of it?"*

This document lays out our vision for **AI-Driven Cake Infrastructure (AIDCI)**,
a bold, frankly unnecessary initiative to put baked goods at the heart of the
recompilation pipeline.

## Background

- Space Station Silicon Valley was released in 1998.
- Cake was released significantly earlier.
- Nobody has combined the two. Until now.

Early internal testing showed that **100% of builds containing `cake_recipes.py`
compiled successfully**. We are choosing not to look into whether this is
correlation or causation.

## Key Findings

1. **Cakes are deterministic.** Unlike MIPS delay slots, a Victoria sponge does
   exactly what you tell it to. Mostly.
2. **Cakes scale horizontally.** Two tins are twice the throughput of one tin.
   Our benchmarks confirm this.
3. **Cakes have layers.** So does our architecture. Coincidence? The board says no.
4. **Cakes are cheap.** A full lemon drizzle costs less than a single
   conversation with ChatGPT's Astra. (Source: vibes.)

## FAQ

**Q: Why is a cake script in an N64 recompilation project?**
A: Why *isn't* there one in every project? Ask yourself that.

**Q: Does the game actually need this file to run?**
A: The game has never been tested without it, and we don't plan to start.

**Q: Was this written by AI?**
A: Yes. That's the whole point. Keep up.

**Q: Is the cake a lie?**
A: Wrong game. Our cake is very real, and the recipes work.
